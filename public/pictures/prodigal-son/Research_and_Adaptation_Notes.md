# Prodigal Son research and adaptation notes

Research completed before screenplay drafting. Source passages and material evidence informed the screenplay; names, ages, gifts, wall, private conversations and exact settings are adaptation choices.

## The biblical narrative

The father divides his livelihood between both sons. Spending precedes famine; pigs belong to the foreign employer. The younger confesses his sin. The father embraces him first, restores him with robe, ring and sandals, and celebrates with the calf. The elder returns from the fields and refuses to enter. The father comes out to him. Luke leaves his answer unstated. This draft preserves that sequence while expanding domestic events. The elder’s allegation about prostitutes remains his allegation, not an independently narrated fact. Biblical dialogue is adapted from the public-domain World English Bible; other dialogue is original.

[Luke 15 1–3 and 11–32](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+15&version=WEB)

## Inheritance and public shame

The firstborn’s double portion supports a two-thirds and one-third division if there are only two sons, but Luke supplies no arithmetic or valuation. The rebellious-son law involves persistent misconduct, parents and elders; it does not prescribe automatic stoning for merely requesting an inheritance. The screenplay does not depict an execution threat or mandatory village expulsion ceremony.

[Deuteronomy 21 15–21](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy+21%3A15-21&version=KJV)

## An early transfer of property

This ancient wisdom text counsels retaining control of property during life. It supports the seriousness of the request without proving that every early transfer was illegal or formally a death wish. The rolled document, silver bundles, stewardship and multi-day settlement in the screenplay are plausible inventions. The father already has portable resources after trading; the film does not require an overnight sale of all farmland. His household authority continues; the restored son receives no second inheritance.

[Sirach 33 19–24](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/sirach/33)

## Animals and food

The pig is prohibited as food. The home keeps cattle and working donkeys; domestic pigs appear only abroad. The pods in the enclosure preserve Luke’s image. The script shows the younger’s desire to eat them without asserting that the text says he did. No maize, potatoes, tomatoes or coffee appear.

[Leviticus 11 7–8](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/leviticus/11)

## Household objects

Dated finds support ceramic cooking and serving vessels, leather sandals with straps, basketry and cordage, wooden and leather objects, limestone cups, combs and silver coin types. These guide object families, not a claim that all Judean households resembled Qumran. The casting, hair lengths, complete garments and exact furnishings remain proposed production choices.

[Israel Antiquities Authority objects at the Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/scrolls/art2.html)

## Textiles

Surviving linen gives material evidence, including stitching; these fragments do not establish an everyday garment pattern. Plain linen tunics and wool mantles are conservative production choices. Muted russet, olive and a narrow blue border distinguish the story’s garments without implying historically mandatory color meanings.

[Qumran textiles at the Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/scrolls/art1.html)

## Writing materials

A dated pottery inkwell supports a restrained writing station. The estate document must be a rolled handwritten prop with script and material reviewed in visual development, never a modern ledger or invented readable legal text presented as authentic.

[Qumran inkwell at the Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/scrolls/art3.html)

## Oil lamps

Use period-appropriate Herodian terracotta lamps with round bodies and flared nozzles. Their light is localized. Avoid later Christian decorations and medieval lighting fixtures. Exact placement and light coverage belong to the later visual and cinematography stages.

[Milwaukee Public Museum lamp classifications](https://www.mpm.edu/index.php/research-collections/anthropology/anthropology-collections-research/mediterranean-oil-lamps/lamp-classifications)
